# Part 91

- **reverberate**
    
    *verb*
    
    to echo repeatedly, to resound, or to have a lasting, far-reaching impact
    
     **The sudden news of the change in leadership reverberated across the whole company.**
    
- **coalesce**
    
    *verb*
    
    DO NOT GET CONFUSED WITH coerce
    
    to fuse or come together to form a single group
    
    **Over time, the various tribes coalesced into a single common culture with one universal language.**
    
- **industrious**
    
    *adjective*
    
    hard-working and persistent
    
    syn: **diligent; assiduous**
    
    🧠 industrious = people in industry are hard working
    
    **Pete was an industrious student, completing every assignment thoroughly and on time.**
    
- **besmirch**
    
    *verb*
    
    to damage someone’s reputation, good name, or honor by making false or harmful statements.
    
    syn: **defame; smear; tarnish; slander**
    
    🧠 besmirch = be+mirch🌶️ = defame
    
    🧠 besmirch = be+smear = tarnish; defame
    
    **The prince's distasteful choice of words besmirched not only his own name but also the reputation of the entire royal family.**
    
- **redress**
    
    *verb*
    
    an act of making something right; to correct, remedy, or compensate for a wrong, injustice, or imbalance.
    
    syn: **rectify; remedy; amend; compensate**
    
    🧠 redress = re-dress the wound = rectify
    
- **implicate**
    
    *verb*
    
    Meaning 1: hint; indicate; convey a meaning; imply
    
    **By saying that some of the guests were uncomfortable, the manager implicated to the hotel staff that it needed to be more diligent.**
    
    🧠 implicate = imply = hint
    
    Meaning 2: to indicate in wrongdoing, usually a crime
    
    **The crime boss was implicated for a long list of crimes, ranging from murder to disturbing the peace.**
    
- **foolhardy**
    
    *adjective*
    
    recklessly bold; taking unnecessary or stupid risks without thinking about the consequences
    
    syn: **reckless; rash; imprudent; heedless(**no heed**)**
    
    🧠 foolhardy = fool + hard = reckless & bold actions

- **tribulation**
    
    *noun*
    
    great suffering, distress, hardship, or a difficult experience over a period of time.
    
    **The family endured years of tribulation after losing their home.**
    
    **Despite many tribulations, she eventually achieved success**

    🧠 tribulation = tribe in trouble = war💥 = the whole tribe experiences severe hardship
    
- **prevail**
    
    *verb*
    
    Meaning 1: to be widespread in certain area in certain time
    
    **During the labor negotiations, an air of hostility prevailed in the office.**
    
    Meaning 2: to succeed, win, or prove more powerful than something else.
    
    **Before the cricket match, Australia was heavily favored, but India prevailed.**
    
    🧠 prevail = pre+victory = someone was a winner previous but now another one has a victory
