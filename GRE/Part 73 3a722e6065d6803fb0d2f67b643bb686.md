# Part 73

- **contingent**
    
    *adjective*
    
    dependent on (usually used with upon or on)
    
    **Success is contingent on hard work.**
    
    **The bonus is contingent upon meeting your sales target.**
    
    ---
    
    *noun*
    
    contingency
    
    future event or circumstance that is possible but uncertain ⇒ usually a negative ones
    
    **In making our business plans, we tried to prepare for any *contingency* that might hurt sales.**
    
- **forthcoming**
    
    *adjective*
    
    Meaning 1: upcoming; happening very soon; approaching
    
    **The president was excited to read the forthcoming bills from senate.**
    
    Meaning 2: ready or helpful when needed; welcoming; being open and cooperative
    
    **He was very forthcoming.**
    
    ant: **unforthcoming = not cooperative**
    
- **ingratiate**
    
    *verb*
    
    to deliberately make yourself liked by someone, usually someone powerful, by flattering them or trying to please them.
    
    Core idea: Win someone's favor through flattery or charm (often for personal gain).
    
    syn: **flatter; curry favor; butter up**
    
    🧠 ingratiate = in-gracias = introduce someone through flattery to win their likeness 
    
    **He tried to ingratiate himself with the manager by constantly praising her.**
    
    **The politician attempted to ingratiate himself with voters by making unrealistic promises.**
    
    **⚠️ The common GRE phrase is:**
    
    > **ingratiate oneself with someone**
    > 
- **impervious**
    
    *adjective*
    
    something that cannot be penetrated, influenced, or affected
    
    syn: **impenetrable;  impermeable**
    
    🧠impervious = im + perv(imagine a pervert that wants to penetrate) = impenetrable
    
- **impermeable**
    
    *adjective*
    
    syn: **impervious; impenetrable**
    
    🧠 impermeable = im + permeate(permeate means penetrate)
    
- **permeate**
    
    *verb*
    
    Meaning 1: to penetrate
    
    Meaning 2: to spread or diffuse through
    
    **a room permeated with tobacco smoke**
    
- **incorrigible**
    
    *adjective*
    
    impossible to correct, reform, or improve, especially because of repeated bad behavior; not able to correct someone even after repeated punishment
    
    syn: refractory;  **irreformable; unmanageable; hopeless**
    
    ant: **corrigible**
    
    🧠 incorrigible = in-correctable
    
    **The teacher called him an incorrigible troublemaker.**
    
    **Despite repeated warnings, the incorrigible liar continued to deceive everyone.**
    
- **refractory**
    
    *adjective*
    
    syn: **incorrigible**
    
- **confound**
    
    *verb*
    
    to cause confusion or perplexity
    
    syn: **dumbfound; dismay; perplex; baffle; bewilder**
    
    🧠 confound = dumbfound
    
- **dumbfound**
    
    *verb*
    
    syn: **confound; dismay; baffle**
    
- **dismay**
    
    *verb*
    
    syn: **confound; dumbfound**
    
- **bumbling**
    
    *adjective*
    
    acting in a clumsy, awkward, or incompetent way, often while trying to do something.
    
    syn: **clumsy, awkward, inept, incompetent, maladroit, blundering, fumbling**
    
    🧠 bumbling = bump+stumbling = clumsy
    
    🧠 bumbling = bumblebee = clumsy
    
- **temperance**
    
    *noun*
    
    self-control, especially in controlling desires, emotions, or consumption ⇒ like avoiding too much spending, too much drinking, etc.
    
    syn: **moderation; restraint; self-control; discipline; self-restraint**
    
    🧠 temperance = controlling temper(emotion) = self-control
    
- **ignoble**
    
    *adjective*
    
    not noble; dishonest; ****dishonorable; shameful
    
    ant: **noble**
    
- **admonitory**
    
    *adjective*
    
    serving as a warn; can cause reproach or punishment
    
    syn: **cautionary; warning**
    
    🧠 admonitory = derived from admonish = can lead to reprimand or reproach to correct someone
    
    **Te high school vice-principal gave the students an admonitory speech, warning them of the many risks and dangers of prom night.**
    
- **monotone**
    
    *noun*
    
    something that is always same and lacks variation
    
    syn: **humdrum; bland**
