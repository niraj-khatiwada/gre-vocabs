# Part 4

- **adulterate**
    
    *verb*
    
    to make something impure or inferior by adding a harmful or unwanted substance
    
    syn: degrade; **debase**; spoil; pollute; corrupt; contaminate
    
    🧠 adulterate ≈ “alter + dirty”
    
    **The milk has been adulterated.**
    
- **debase**
    
    *verb*
    
    reduce the quality or value of something
    
    syn: **adulterate; degrade**
    
- **aggrandize**
    
    *verb*
    
    to increase power, status, wealth, or importance
    
    syn: **augment; magnify**
    
    🧠 aggrandize = make grand(big)
    
    **The ruler sought to aggrandize himself through grand monuments.**
    
- **alacrity**
    
    *adjective*
    
    promptness in response **:** cheerful readiness; eagerness/willingness to do something quickly
    
    syn: **willingness**; **fervor**
    
    🧠 Alacrity terminal
    
    **Accepted the invitation with *alacrity.***
    
- **ambivalent**
    
    *adjective*
    
    having mixed or conflicting feelings about something; unable to decide because you feel both positive and negative at the same time
    
    syn: **unsure**; **equivocal; wavering; indecisive**
    
    🧠
    
    - **ambi-** = both
    - **valent** = feeling / strength (same root as *value*)
    
    ---
    
    - **equi** = equal
    - **vocal** = voice/saying
    
    👉 “equal voices” → multiple possible meanings → **uncertain**
    
    **It’s normal to feel ambivalent when moving to a new country.**
    
- **waver**
    
    *verb*
    
    *waver & falter has 2 distinct meanings*
    
    Meaning 1: To go back & forth = indecisive; hesitate
    
    syn: **ambivalent; equivocal**
    
    🧠 waver = waves 🏄 = goes back & forth
    
    ---
    
    Meaning 2: To lose strength, confidence or momentum
    
- **falter**
    
    *verb*
    
    syn: waver
    
- **unequivocal**
    
    *adjective*
    
    completely clear, **unambiguous**, and leaving no doubt; absolute
    
    ant: **equivocal; absolute; lucid**
    
- **ameliorate**
    
    *verb*
    
    to make something better, improved, or less severe, especially a bad or difficult situation
    
    syn: **mitigate; remedy; ease**
    
    🧠 **melior** = better (Latin root) | **a- / am-** = toward
    
    **GitHub provided 1 month free premium membership to ameliorate the week long downtime services.**
    
- **amenable**
    
    DO NOT GET CONFUSED WITH amiable
    
    *adjective*
    
    **openness**/**willingness** to change or agree or accept something; willing to co-operate
    
    syn: **compliant**; **acquiescent**
    
    **🧠 amenable ≈ “I am able (to agree)”
    The president was not amenable on his decision to end the war.**
    
- **acquiescent**
    
    *adjective*
    
    - Willing to accept something without protest
    - Submissive; quietly agreeing, often without enthusiasm = **“Fine… whatever you say.”**
    - Not resisting, even if not fully approving
    
    syn: **amenable**
    
    🧠: **ACQUIES-cent ≈ QUIT resisting =** When someone stops fighting back and just goes along, they become **acquiescent**.
    
- **anachronistic**
    
    *adjective*
    
    out of place in time; out of an era/period
    
    syn: outdated; out-of-date; **outmoded**
    
    🧠 **ana-** = against / backward + **chron** = time (like *chronology*)
    
    **Dreams of colonial conquest is anachronistic in today’s age.**
    
    ---
    
    *noun*
    
    anachronism
    
- **outmoded**
    
    *adjective*
    
    outdated; old-fashioned
    
- **avaricious**
    
    *adjective*
    
    greedy; always wanting more
    
    syn: **rapacious; acquisitive (think like acquisition)**
    
    🧠 a very vicious greed
    
    **The avaricious businessman exploited every opportunity for profit.**
    
- **rapacious**
    
    *adjective*
    
    syn: **avaricious**
    
- **acquisitive**
    
    *adjective*
    
    syn: **avaricious; rapacious**
    
    🧠 acquisitive = too many acquisition (like a business person) = greedy
    
- **salutary**
    
    *adjective*
    
    having a beneficial or positive effect
    
    syn: **salubrious**
    
    🧠 **salut-** relates to **health / well-being** (same root idea as “salute” → good wishes)
    
    **Some celebrities have salutary influences on teens.**
    
- **salubrious**
    
    *adjective*
    
    syn: **salutary**
    
- **spurious**
    
    *adjective*
    
    false, misleading, or not genuine; something that appears true but is actually based on deception or faulty reasoning
    
    syn: **counterfeit; fake; forged; deceptive; specious**
    
    🧠 **spurious ≈ “suspicious”** 
    
    🧠 **specious = “suspicious”**
    
    **The article was based on spurious assumptions.**
    
- **specious**
    
    *adjective*
    
    seems plausible but is fake; suspicious
    
    syn: **spurious**
    
- **peculiar**
    
    DO NOT GET CONFUSED WITH perilous
    
    *adjective*
    
    strange, unusual, or different from what is normal or expected
    
    🧠 **peculiar ≈ “particular + weird”**
    
    **peculiar demand**
    
    **peculiar face**
    
- **treacherous**
    
    *adjective*
    
    1. someone disloyal who betrays trust; 
    2. something marked by hidden dangers, hazards, or perils
    
    🧠 **treacherous → traitor**
    
    🧠 **treacherous → dangerous → perilous →** (imagine someone betrayed and now your situation is in danger)
    
    **A *treacherous* act of betrayal.**
    
    **They were not prepared to hike over such *treacherous* terrain.**
    
- **vindicated**
    
    *verb*
    
    to be cleared of blame, suspicion, or doubt, typically by proving that a previous accusation was unfair or that a person was right after all
    
    🧠 vindicate = win + indicate = means i was doubtful at first, but now it’s clear that i can win 
    
    🧠 Think:
    
    > “I was right all along.”
    > 
    
    That feeling = **vindication**
    
    **My continual disgust at the JS ecosystem feels vindicated practically daily at this point.**
    
- **snub**
    
    *verb*
    
     to deliberately ignore or treat someone with cold, disdainful disrespect; insult
    
    **James Rodriguez snubbed daughter of Columbian president when asked for selfie.**
    
- **castigate**
    
    *verb*
    
    criticize, disapprove, scold, or punish someone severely or harshly, often publicly
    
    **The judge *castigated* the lawyers for their lack of preparation.**
    
    🧠 Castle + gate = guard punishes you strongly if you breach the gate
    
- **pillory**
    
    *noun/verb*
    
    to subject (someone) to harsh public criticism
    
    syn: **censure, reprimand,** condemn, denounce, criticize
    
    Pillory, Censure and Reprimand are all forms of condemnation but they differ in severity.
    
    Pillory/Castigate: Harshest
    
    Censure/Reprimand: Strongest
    
    Chide/Rebuke: Mildest
    
- **censure**
    
    *noun/verb*
    
    a judgment involving condemnation; the act of blaming or criticizing
    
- **reprimand**
    
    *noun/verb*
    
- **chide**
    
    *verb*
    
    > Same as reprimand but this is the mildest form of criticizing
    > 
- **chastise**
    
    *verb*
    
    syn: **reprimand; chide; rebuke**
    
- **rebuke**
    
    *verb*
    
    public form of disapproval; (mildest just like chide)
    
    syn: **reprimand**, condemn, criticize, censure
    
    > Calm and firm disapproval without rage.
    > 
    
    Summary
    
    - castigate; pillory= Harshest
    - reprimand; censure = milder (on stronger side)
    - chide; rebuke = mildest
- **homogeneity**

    syn: **uniformity**
    
    🧠 homogeneous = similar

    *adjective*

    **ant: spendthrift; thriftless**
    
    REMEMBER: **Spendthrift means spending recklessly but thrifty means spending carefully**
    
- **frugal**
    
    to be wise with your money or resources; avoiding waste
    
    syn: **economical, thrifty**
    
    ant: **prodigal**; **profligate**
    
    REMEMBER: **prodigal means spending recklessly but frugal means spending carefully**
