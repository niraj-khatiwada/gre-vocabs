- **berate**
    
    *verb*
    
    scold or criticize harshly
    
    syn: **castigate; chastise; assail**
    
- **glib**
    
    *adjective*
    
    speaking smoothly and confidently but without sincerity, thought, or depth.
    
    syn: **facile**; **superficial; slick; smooth-talking**
    
    > A glib person has easy words, but shallow ideas. “**The politician gave a glib answer that sounded good but solved nothing.**”
    > 
    
    🧠 glib = glee(happy) + blabber(talking nonsense) = "**Don't worry! Everything will be perfect! I have all the answers!**”
    
- **facile**
    
    *adjective*
    
    done with ease but not care or effort or depth
    
    Core Idea: done surfacely without any depth
    
    syn: **glib**
    
    🧠 facile = super**ficial**
    
- **fickle**
    
    *adjective*
    
    syn: **capricious; mercurial**
    
    🧠 fickle = flip-flop = keep changing = mercurial
    
- **capricious**
    
    *adjective (caprice is noun)*
    
    having varying moods; **fickle**
    
    syn: **mercurial(***remember: mercury is volatile***)**; **erratic; fickle**
    
    🧠 Capricious = Caprice = a sudden impulse or whim = varying mood
    
    **Inviting our capricious/mercurial brother over the weekend is risky.**
    
- **mercurial**
    
    *noun*
    
    volatile
    
    🧠 *Mercury = volatile/unstable → mercurial*
    
- **perennial**
    
    *adjective*
    
    syn: **perpetual; everlasting; ceaseless**
    
    🧠 perennial = perpetual
    
    🧠 perennial = permanent + annual
    
- **debase**
    
    *verb*
    
    reduce the quality or value of something
    
    syn: **adulterate; degrade**
    
    🧠 debase = de+base = remove the base = degrade = reduce the quality
    
- **adulterate**
    
    *verb*
    
    to make something impure or inferior by adding a harmful or unwanted substance
    
    syn: **degrade; debase; spoil; pollute; corrupt; contaminate**
    
    🧠 adulterate ≈ “alter + dirty”
    
    **The milk has been adulterated.**
    
- **smattering**
    
    *noun*
    
    a small, superficial amount of knowledge or something
    
    🧠 smattering = small+scattered = little knowledge = imagine a foreign language
    
    **He has a smattering of French.
    → He knows a few French words, but he is not fluent.**
    
    **She has a smattering of knowledge about economics.
    → She knows a little, but not deeply.**
    
    **The book contains a smattering of humor.
    → It contains a small amount of humor.**
    
- **inadvertent**
    
    *adjective*
    
    happening by chance or unexpectedly or unintentionally
    
    syn: **heedless; accidental; unintentional**
    
    ant: **advertent** = intentional
    
    🧠 inadvertent = in+advertent = imagine advertent means you intentionally post an advert(ad) = so inadvertent means accidental/unintentional
    
    - **The email was sent due to an inadvertent mistake.
    → It was sent accidentally.**
    - **She made an inadvertent error in the calculation.
    → She did not mean to make the error.**
    - **His inadvertent comment offended someone.
    → He did not intend to offend them.**
- **elaborate**

    adjective = complex and detailed
    
    verb = to explain in detail
    
- **cornucopia**
    
    *noun*

    syn: **plethora; abundance**
    
    🧠 cornucopia = corn+copious = large amount of corn
