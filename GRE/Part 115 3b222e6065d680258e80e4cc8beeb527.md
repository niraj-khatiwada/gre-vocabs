# Part 115

- **contumacious**
    
    *adjective*
    
    stubbornly refusing to obey authority, especially in a disrespectful or rebellious way.
    
    🧠 contumacious = continue+massie = T. Massie refuses to obey Tr** = refusing to obey authority
    
    **The judge punished the contumacious witness for refusing to answer questions.
    → The witness deliberately disobeyed the court.**
    
    **The prisoner was contumacious and ignored the guards' orders.
    → He was rebelliously uncooperative.**
    
    **The employee's contumacious behavior led to his dismissal.
    → He openly refused to follow instructions.**
    
- **recalcitrant**
    
    *adjective*
    
    stubbornly resistant to authority, control
    
    🧠 recalcitrant = reject+call+tyrant  = reject call from authority
    
    **The recalcitrant student refused to follow the teacher's instructions.
    → The student resisted authority.**
    
    **The company faced a recalcitrant employee who ignored every rule.
    → An uncooperative employee.**
    
    **The horse was recalcitrant and refused to move.
    → It resisted control.**

- **incipient**

    **Their friendship showed signs of an incipient romance.

- **requite**
    
    *verb*
    
    to return or repay something, especially a feeling, action, or favor.
    
    Core idea: Give back what was given.
    
    syn: **reciprocate; repay**
    
    🧠 requite = **re**turn+**qui**ck favor = repay the favor
    
    **She requited his love.
    → She returned his feelings.**
    
    **He helped me, and I hope to requite his kindness.
    → I want to repay his kindness.**
    
    **The country requited the aid it received.
    → It returned the favor.**
