# Part 90

- **presumption**
    
    *noun*
    
    Meaning 1: an assumption based on probability or reasonable evidence
    
    Core idea: a reasonable assumption until proven otherwise.
    
    **There is a presumption of innocence in many legal systems.
    → A person is assumed innocent until proven guilty.**
    
    **The decision was based on the presumption that demand would increase.
    → It was based on a reasonable assumption.**
    
    ---
    
    Meaning 2: excessive confidence; boldness that goes beyond what is appropriate
    
    Core idea: arrogant overconfidence.
    
    **His presumption angered his colleagues.
    → His arrogant behavior offended them.**
    
    **It was sheer presumption to tell the CEO how to run the company on his first day.
    → He was overly bold and disrespectful.**
    
- **travail**
    
    *noun/verb*
    
    painful, exhausting effort or labor; intense suffering
    
    🧠 travail = travel+ailment = travel with pain = arduous
    
    **After years of travail, she finally completed her PhD.
    → Years of difficult struggle and hard work.**
    
    **The refugees endured great travail during their journey.
    → They suffered greatly.**
    
    **The company emerged stronger after a period of financial travail.
    → A period of severe hardship.**
    
- **maxim**
    
    *noun*
    
    a short saying expressing a general truth or wisdom
    
    syn: **aphorism; proverb; saying; motto; principle**
    
    🧠 maxim = maximum wisdom in minimum words
    
    **"Honesty is the best policy" is a well-known maxim.**
    
    **She lived by the maxim, "Actions speak louder than words."**
    
    **The teacher began each class with a maxim about learning.**

- **brusquely**
    
    *adverb*
    
    in a blunt, direct manner (usually in a rude way)
    
    🧠 brusque = brush someone off = *uta jaa 😠*
    
    **"Leave me alone," he said brusquely.
    → He spoke in a harsh, unfriendly way.**
    
    **The manager brusquely rejected the proposal.
    → The manager rejected it quickly and bluntly.**
    
    **She answered my question brusquely and walked away.
    → Her response was short and somewhat rude.**
    
- **credulity**
    
    *noun*
    
    a tendency to believe things too easily, often without enough evidence
    
    🧠 credulity = credible? Okay👍
    
    🧠 credulity = derived from noun cred which means trust, credibility
    
    **The scam succeeded because it exploited people's credulity.
    → People believed the false claims too easily.**
    
    **His credulity made him accept every rumor he heard.
    → He was too trusting.**
    
    **The magician relies on the audience's credulity.
    → The audience's willingness to believe helps the trick work.**

- **leery**
    
    *adjective*
    
    cautious, suspicious, or unwilling to trust something because you think it may be risky or unreliable.
    
    🧠 leery = look(👀)+eerie = you look suspicious because of eerie situation(risky & unreliable)
    
    syn: **wary; chary**
    
    **She was leery of investing in the unknown company.
    → She was suspicious and cautious.**
    
    **I am leery of online deals that seem too good to be true.
    → I don't trust them.**
    
    **The dog was leery of strangers.
    → The dog was cautious around unfamiliar people.**

- **taxing**
    
    *adjective*
    
    requiring a lot of effort, energy, or mental ability; very demanding.
    
    🧠 taxing = paying tax = requires a lot of effort on tax day
    
    **Writing a thesis is a taxing process.
    → It requires a lot of effort.**
    
    **The job was physically and mentally taxing.
    → The job was exhausting.**
    
    **Solving the puzzle was intellectually taxing.
    → It required a lot of thinking.**
    
- **foible**
    
    *noun*
    
    a small weakness, fault, or unusual habit in someone's character.
    
    syn: **quirk; idiosyncrasy; flaw**
    
    🧠 foible = **f**law+fee**ble** = a person’s flaw or weakness
    
    **Everyone has their own foibles.
    → Everyone has small imperfections.**
    
    **His habit of checking his phone every five minutes is a harmless foible.
    → A small personal weakness or quirk.**
    
    **The novel portrays the characters with all their strengths and foibles.
    → Their good qualities and minor flaws.**
