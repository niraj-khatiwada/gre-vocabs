# Part 108

- **gambit**
    
    *noun*
    
    a clever action or strategy designed to gain an advantage, often involving some risk or sacrifice.
    
    🧠 gambit = queen’s gambit = clever strategy to win the chess game
    
    **The politician's opening statement was a clever gambit to win public support.
    → A calculated strategy.**
    
    **Offering a discount was a gambit to attract new customers.
    → A tactic to gain an advantage.**
    
    **The chess player sacrificed a pawn as a gambit.
    → A strategic sacrifice for future advantage.**
    
- **languish**
    
    *verb*
    
    Meaning 1: to become weak or lethargic
    
    Meaning 2: to be in a unpleasant condition due to lethargy for a long time ⇒ suffering
    
    🧠 languish = verb form of languish (🧠 remember langur?)
    
    **The project languished because nobody provided funding.
    → It remained inactive and failed to progress.**
    
    **The prisoner languished in jail for years.
    → He suffered by remaining there.**
    
    **The old building has languished without repairs.
    → It has deteriorated over time.**
    
    **The plant began to languish without enough sunlight.
    → It became weak.**

- **atavism**
    
    *noun*
    
     a reappearance of an earlier characteristic or traits
    
    Core Idea: a throwback to the past
    
    syn: **throwback, reversion, regression, recurrence, revival, remnant**
    
    🧠 atavism = a trace of ancestor's version = has traits from ancestors = throwback 
    
    **The extra toe in the animal was an atavism.

    **His violent behavior was described as an atavism of a more primitive era.
    → A return to an older, less civilized tendency.**
    
    **The old ritual was an atavism that survived into modern times.
    → A leftover from the past.**

    **The debate recrudesced after new evidence appeared.
    → The debate started again.**
