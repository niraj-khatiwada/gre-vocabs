# Part 2

- **deify**
    
    *verb*
    
    to treat someone like a **god**, worship them, or admire/honor them excessively
    
    sync: **beatify; sanctify | revere (**think of reverence**)**, **venerate ⇒ in terms of respect**
    
    🧠 dei → deo → god
    
    🧠 derived from **deity =** A deity is a supernatural being, such as a god or goddess, worshipped for believed control over the world or life aspects (like Jesus)
    
    **Materialistic people who *deify* money.**
    
- **beatify**
    
    *verb*
    
    syn: **deify; sanctify**
    
- **sanctify**
    
    *verb*
    
    syn: **deify; beatify**
    
- **reverence**
    
    *noun*
    
    show deep respect or honor; a gesture of respect (such as a bow)
    
    **Your reverence towards Pope…**
    
    ---
    
    *verb*
    
    revere
    
- **homage**
    
    *noun*
    
    honor; deep respect or admiration and often praise shown for a person or god
    
    syn: **reverence**
    
    **pay homage**
    
    Also, homage means to honor someone by copying/imitating their life’s work in a book or film. So, GRE uses this word for matching with copying/imitating as well
    
    **The film is an homage to his life’s work.**
    
- **didactic**
    
    *adjective*
    
    meant for teaching or educational purpose
    
    sync: **instructional**, **pedagogic**
    
    🧠 **didaktikos = apt at teaching**
    
    **The film’s primary objective is didactic.**
    
- **pedagogic**
    
    *adjective*
    
    syn: **instructive; didactic**
    
    🧠 pedagogic = padne 📖
    
- **flout**
    
    *verb*
    
    to openly disregard, mock, or defy a rule, law
    
    sync: **defy, disdain, disregard**
    
    🧠 flout = flaunt rules
    
    **Students flouted the new bathroom break rules.**
    
    - **pedagogic**
        
        *adjective*
        
        syn: **instructive; didactic**
        
- **humdrum**
    
    *adjective*
    
    boring; unlikely to excite; lacking originality
    
    syn: **dull**, **mundane, banal**
    
    🧠 “hum… drum… hum… drum…” → repeated too often → that’s why its boring
    
    **Netflix movies are humdrum.**
    
- **banal**
    
    unexciting because it’s repeated too often; unoriginal
    
    syn: **trite**, **hackneyed**
    
- **trite**
    
    unoriginal or lacking idea
    
    syn: banal
    
- **hackneyed**
    
    *adjective*
    
    syn: **banal, trite; humdrum**
    
    🧠 hackneyed = hack + neyed(again) = hacked again and again(repeated) = banal
    
- **insipid**
    
    *adjective*
    
    lacking flavor, interest or energy; lifeless
    
    syn: **bland**, plain, **vapid, anemic**
    
    **Insipid concert**
    
- **vapid**
    
    *adjective*
    
    syn: insipid, anemic, dull, lifeless
    
- **anemic**
    
    *adjective*
    
    syn: **insipid**
    
    🧠 Derived from **anemia**: Anemia means lack of red blood cell → plain; dull
    
- **pallid**
    
    *adjective*
    
    pale, lacking color, or lacking vitality/energy.
    
    syn: **anemic; insipid; vapid**
    
    🧠 pallid = pale+lid = a lid with no color🌕
    
    **Her face became pallid after hearing the shocking news.
    → Her face became pale.**
    
    **The room was decorated with pallid colors.
    → Weak, faded colors.**
    
    **The speech was a pallid imitation of the original.
    → Dull and lacking strength.**
    
- **loquacious**
    
    *adjective*
    
    likely to talk more; wordy; verbose
    
    syn: **talkative; voluble (**remember big volume**); garrulous**
    
    🧠 Imagine someone:
    
    - answers a yes/no question
    - then talks for 20 minutes
    
    That person is **loquacious**.
    
- **garrulous**
    
    *adjective*
    
- **misanthropic**
    
    *adjective*
    
    having distaste for humans & society
    
    syn: **reclusive**; antisocial
    
    🧠 Anthropic company
    
    **Misanthropic AI data-center**
    
- **reclusive**
    
    *adjective*
    
    lives in solitude or seclusion; retiring from society; antisocial
    
    🧠 reclusive = seclusion = who lives in secluded region or solidarily
    
- **tantalizing**
    
    *adjective*
    
    tempting or arousing interest or stimulating curiosity
    
    🧠 **A tantalizing job offer.**
    
- **axiom**
    
    *adjective*
    
    rule, or principle accepted as true without proof
    
    🧠 Axioms in math = an *axiom* is an unprovable rule or first principle accepted as true because it is self-evident
    
    For eg: Through any two distinct points, there is exactly one straight line.
    
- **nuisance**
    
    *adjective*
    
    *Do not get confused with nuance*
    
    one that is annoying, unpleasant, or obnoxious; can cause inconvenience or irritation
    
    **My allergies are a *nuisance on summer.***
    
    🧠 nuisance: no sense = something does something that makes no sense and makes you unpleasant
    
    Your neighbor starts mowing the lawn at **5 AM**.
    
    You think:
    
    > **"This makes no sense!"**
    > 
    
    That annoying situation is a **nuisance**.
    
- **vex**
    
    *verb*
    
    Meaning 1: to bring trouble or distress or frustration and agitation
    
    Meaning 2: this also means to cause petty annoyance to someone
    
    🧠 vex = v + ex → **v**ery **ex**treme → extreme trouble or distress
    
    **This problem has *vexed* researchers for years. ⇒ caused trouble for researchers**
    
- **exasperate**
    
    *verb*
    
    DO NOT GET CONFUSED WITH exacerbate
    
    an intense feeling of annoyance or anger or agitation or frustration
    
    syn: **vex**
    
    🧠 exasperate = exacerbate the anger
    
- **extenuating**
    
    *verb*
    
    circumstances that can be considered less severe/serious or forgivable
    
    *always used words like circumstances, conditions, etc.*
    
    syn: **mitigating; excusing; alleviating; moderating; pacifying; palliating**
    
    **The university will waive off the entrance fees for students with extenuating circumstances.**
