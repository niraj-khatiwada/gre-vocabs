# Part 89

- **destitute**
    
    *adjective*
    
    lacking; wanting (can be money or any other resources)
    
    syn: **penurious; parsimonious; tightfisted**
    
    🧠 destitute = **des**perate+pros**titute** = lacking money
    
    **After losing his job and home, he became destitute.
    → He had almost nothing.**
    
    **The charity helps destitute families.
    → Families living in extreme poverty.**
    
    **The war left thousands of people destitute.
    → People were left without basic needs.**
    
- **penurious**
    
    *adjective*
    
    extremely stingy or unwilling to spend money; also, very poor or lacking resources.
    
    syn: **parsimonious; stingy; tightfisted; impoverished; destitute; needy**
    
    🧠 penurious = penny + furious = stingy
    
    ---
    
    *noun*
    
    penury
    
- **parsimonious**
    
    *adjective*
    
    syn: **penurious; destitute**
    
- **indigent**
    
    *adjective*
    
    someone who is extremely poor and lacking the basic necessities of life
    
    🧠 indigent = in dire need of gain = extremely poor
    
- **devolve**
    
    *verb*
    
    Meaning 1: opposite of evolve(evolution) = worsen
    
    **The dialogue between the two academics devolved into a downright bitter argument.**
    
    Meaning 2: delegate task = pass the tasks
    
    **The company was full of managers known for devolving tasks to lower management, but never doing much work themselves.**
    
- **exasperate**
    
    *verb*
    
    DO NOT GET CONFUSED WITH exacerbate
    
    an intense feeling of annoyance or anger or agitation or frustration
    
    🧠 exasperate = exacerbate the anger
    
- **aphoristic**
    
    *adjective*
    
    expressed in a short, clever, memorable statement that conveys a general truth or wisdom
    
    Core Idea: short + wise + memorable.
    
    🧠 aphoristic
    
    aphoristic = A phrase of wisdom = short, concise statement of a wisdom
    
    Think:
    
    A-phoristic → A phrase
    
    A phrase that contains wisdom:
    
    "Actions speak louder than words."
    
    That is an aphorism.
    
    ---
    
    *noun*
    
    aphorism
    
- **collusion**
    
    *noun*
    
    a secret agreement for deceitful mission
    
    🧠 collusion = derived from verb collude = 🧠 collaborate + delude(deceive) = collaborate to create a deceptive plan
    
    **The detective discovered that the two politicians were in collusion to manipulate the election results.**

- **eke**
    
    *verb*
    
    to manage to live on meager resources ⇒ to last long by consuming less resource
    
    🧠 eke = extend + keep = last longer by consuming less
    
    **Stranded in a cabin over the winter, Terry was able to eke out an existence on canned food.**
    
- **rash**
    
    *adjective*
    
    acting hastily without aforethought
    
    🧠 rash = it was a rash decision
    
- **quip**
    
    *noun/verb*
    
    a clever, witty, or sarcastic remark, usually made quickly.
    
    🧠 quip = quick + punchline = comedian makes quick punchline
    
    **He is famous for his witty quips during interviews.
    → Clever, funny remarks.**
    
    **She quipped that the meeting could have been an email.
    → She made a quick humorous comment.**
    
    **The comedian's quip made everyone laugh.
    → A short joke.**

- **savvy**
    
    *noun/adjective/verb*
    
    having practical knowledge, good judgment, and the ability to understand
    
    **She is tech-savvy and can fix most computer problems.
    → She understands technology well.**
    
    **A savvy investor knows when to take risks.
    → A practical, knowledgeable investor.**
    
    **He is politically savvy.
    → He understands politics and knows how to navigate it.**
    
- **ploy**
    
    *verb*
    
    a clever plot to gain advantage
    
    🧠 ploy = plot
    
    **The advertisement was just a ploy to attract customers.
    → A tactic designed to get attention.**
    
    **Pretending to be friendly was a ploy to gain his trust.
    → A planned trick.**
    
    **The politician's promise was seen as an election ploy.
    → A strategy to win votes.**
    
- **tact**
    
    *noun*
    
    Meaning 1: skilled in handling situation logically or with tactics
    
    syn: **diplomacy; discretion**
    
    🧠 tact = derived from tactics = logic
    
    Meaning 2: the ability to say or do things in a sensitive way without offending or upsetting others ⇒ having etiquette and decorum
    
    syn: **elegant; prudence; finesse;**
    
    🧠 tact = talk with tact = great etiquette & decorum
    
- **elude**
    
    *verb*
    
    to escape, avoid, or fail to be understood ⇒ same as evade but usually elude is used where <person> escapes by using cleverness
    
    syn: **evade**
    
    **“The infamous art thief has eluded the police once again.”**
    
    **“He tried to elude the security personnel by sneaking through the back door.”**
    
    ---
    
    *adjective*
    
    elusive
    
- **tout**
    
    *verb*
    
    to praise, promote, or advertise something aggressively, especially to get attention or sales.
    
    🧠 tout = shout 📢 + promote
    
    **The company touted its new AI technology as revolutionary.
    → The company strongly promoted it.**
    
    **The athlete was touted as the next great champion.
    → People praised and advertised his potential.**
    
    **The advertisement touted the product's benefits.
    → It highlighted the product to attract buyers.**
    
- **reservation**
    
    *noun*
    
    doubt or hesitation; skepticism
    
    🧠 reservation = reserve your judgement
    
    **I was initially excited by the idea of a trip to Washington, D.C. but now that I have read about the high crime statistics there, I have some reservations.**
    
- **perturb**
    
    *verb*
    
    to disturb, worry, or make someone anxious or unsettled or alarmed
    
    syn: **unsettle; distress; alarm;’ disturb**
    
    🧠 perturb = person’s turbulence = disturb someone = make someone unsettled
    
    **Now that Henry is recovering from a major illness, he no longer lets the little trivialities, such as late mail, perturb him.**
