- **predicament**
    
    *noun*
    
    an unpleasant or embarrassing situation that is difficult to get out of
    
    **“The club’s financial predicament.”**
    
    **“With no money and job, he found himself in a real predicament.”**
    
- **prolixity**
    
    *noun*
    
    verbose; using excessive words
    
    syn: **verbosity; volubility**
    
    🧠 prolixity = derived from prolix (🧠 remember prolix?) = pro lips = talking too much = verbose
    
- **subordinate**
    
    *noun/adjective*
    
    having a lower rank; having little importance
    
    syn: **inferior; peripheral**
    
    🧠 subordinate = sub = less = lower
    
- **veritable**
    
    *adjective*
    
    true; genuine
    
    syn: **legitimate; undeniable; indisputable**
    
    🧠 veritable = derived from word verity which means true or factual or genuine
    
- **promulgate**
    
    *verb*
    
    to officially announce or declare or publish
    
    🧠 **prom**ulg**ate** = promote + circulate
    
- **ruffle**
    
    *verb*
    
    to disturb, annoy, upset, or distress someone
    
    🧠 ruffle = rough+scuffle = scuffle means a messy fight = disturb or distress
    
    **The criticism did not ruffle her confidence.
    → It did not upset her.**
    
    **The wind ruffled his hair.
    → It made his hair messy.**
    
    **The unexpected question ruffled the politician.
    → It disturbed his composure.**
